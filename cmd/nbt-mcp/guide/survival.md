# Survival style

A survival-style build is one a player could believably make on a normal survival world: practical, made of common materials, with detail where it counts. It should feel earned, not lavish.

## Materials

- Use blocks that are cheap and gathered early: oak, spruce, birch and dark oak logs and planks, cobblestone, mossy cobblestone, stone, stone bricks, andesite, diorite, granite, gravel, dirt, coarse dirt, sand, sandstone, glass and glass panes, wool, hay bales, bricks (a little), terracotta from the local biome.
- Match the biome: spruce and cobblestone in taigas, sandstone and smooth sandstone in deserts, oak and stone bricks on plains, mud bricks and packed mud near swamps.
- Avoid or keep very rare: quartz, prismarine, purpur, end stone, concrete, glazed terracotta, copper (except a few accents), amethyst, any mineral block (gold, diamond, emerald, lapis, netherite), sea lanterns, beacons, nether materials outside the Nether.
- Lighting: torches and lanterns. A few campfires. No end rods or froglights.

## Palette size and detail

- 3 to 6 distinct block types on the exterior is plenty. One main wall block, one frame wood, one roof block, one foundation block, and one accent.
- Detail blocks (stairs, slabs, fences, trapdoors) around 8 to 15% of the visible surface. Put them where a player would: window sills, roof edges, the door step, a fence railing.
- Weathering is natural: a few mossy and cracked variants in stone, but not elaborate gradients.

## Scale and layout

- Small and functional: houses of 7x7 to 13x13, one or two storeys. Farms, animal pens, a storage room, a nether portal frame.
- Shapes are simpler: a rectangle with a porch, an L-shaped house, a lean-to. A roof that overhangs by 1 block. Symmetry is fine but a chimney, shed or log pile should break it.
- Interiors are practical: chests, barrels, crafting table, furnaces, bed, a few item frames.
